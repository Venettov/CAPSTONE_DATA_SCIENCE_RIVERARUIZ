Graduate Capstone Project

Population & Economic Dynamics in Puerto Rico

A graduate research project examining demographic shifts, out-migration, and sustainable development strategies through linked data analysis and policy synthesis.

 

Integrated Data Architecture

Data Category Source Variables (Features) Why it’s Appropriate
Target Variable U.S. Census PEP (Population Estimates Program) • Total Population
• Net Migration 		This is the Ground Truth variable we are trying to predict.
Demographic Structure U.S. Census / Vital Statistics • Age Structure (% under 15, % over 65)
• Crude Birth Rate 		These proxy demographic momentum — the internal speed of decline regardless of hurricanes.
Economic Resilience ACS (American Community Survey) & County Business Patterns • Unemployment Rate
• Payroll Counts
• Establishment Counts 		Economic health acts as a “buffer.” Towns with jobs may retain people better after a storm.
Social Vulnerability CDC SVI (Social Vulnerability Index) • Poverty Rate, Single Parent
• Transportation / % No Vehicle
• % Disabled, etc. 		15 census-based variables capturing vulnerability exposure.
External Shocks NOAA & USGS • Hurricane Strike (Binary/Categorical)
• Earthquake Intensity 		Allows the model to distinguish “shock years” (2017, 2020) from “normal years.”
Safety & Security Puerto Rico Police Bureau • Violent and Property Crime Rates per capita Controls for “Safety-Driven Displacement.”

Data Viability & Limitations

  • Viability:

    All data is publicly available, government-certified, and covers the exact same geographic boundaries (78 Municipalities of Puerto Rico)

  • Limitation:

    Migration data at the town/municipality level can be "noisy" (volatile)

  • Mitigation:

    Smooth this noise using Regularization techniques (Ridge/Lasso) that penalize outliers.

Pipeline Risk Assessment & Mitigation

Risk Identified Level Mitigation Plan
Data Volatility ("Noise"):
Migration data at the town level can be noisy.
Medium
Regularization:
Utilize Lasso (L1) and Ridge regression techniques to penalize outliers and force the model to focus only on significant drivers.
Non-Linear Shocks:
Standard linear models may fail to predict the "cliff-like" population drops caused by disasters
Medium
Non-Linear Algorithms:
Use Random Forest and Gradient Boosting to capture abrupt shifts that traditional demographic tools (DAPPS) miss
Dataset Alignment:
Harmonizing time-series data from 5+ disparate sources (Census, NOAA, Police Bureau, etc.)
Medium
ETL Pipeline:
Implement a reproducible Python/Pandas pipeline to standardize FIPS codes and temporal resolution into a single municipal-level panel
Model Generalization:
Risk of the model overfitting to a specific "shock" year (like Maria)
Low
Validation Strategy:
Train in 2010–2016 data and test specifically on the 2017 "Maria crash" to verify the model's ability to interpret shocks
